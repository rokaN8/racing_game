# Glossary

**Bike** — The single player-controlled vehicle. Leaves a light trail (visual only).

**Track** — One closed course the Bike races, with its own layout, palette, Song and High scores. Can twist, bank, and form Loops; may contain Gaps.

**Loop** — A section of Track that curves fully vertical, carrying the Bike upside down and back.

**Wall-ride** — A stretch of Track banked to roughly vertical, ridden sideways by Track-relative gravity.

**Corkscrew** — A straight stretch of Track that rolls a full 360° around its centre line, carrying the Bike upside down and back upright. Built with the turtle's Corkscrew piece; each Track picks its length, roll direction and how long it holds upside down.

**Double Loop** — Two Loops one after the other, the second offset sideways from the first, either back to back or with a short straight between.

**Helix** — A turn that carries on past a full circle while climbing or falling steadily, so its coils stack one above another: a spiral climb or descent. Each Track picks its radius, bank, how many times round and how high.

**Gap** — A break in the Track the Bike must jump across while Airborne.

**Kicker** — The ramp before a Gap that lifts the Bike into the air at the lip.

**Landing deck** — The flat Track after a Gap, long enough that every speed which clears the Gap lands on it.

**Landing grade** — How cleanly the Bike came down, judged by how far it was tilted from the Track it landed on: Perfect, Clean or Sketchy. Tilted further than Sketchy is a crash.

**Perfect landing** — A landing square to the Track, the best Landing grade. After a Gap it gives a short Boost.

**Rocket start** — Pressing the throttle just before GO, so the Bike launches off the line already moving.

**Stall** — Holding the throttle too long before GO: the Bike bogs down and goes nowhere for a moment after GO.

**Track-relative gravity** — While on the Track, gravity pulls the Bike into the Track surface beneath it, not world-down. This is what lets the Bike ride walls and Loops.

**Cornering pull** — The sideways push towards a turn's outside edge while on the Track. The player steers against it; it grows with speed and is softened by banking.

**Airborne** — The Bike is off the Track (e.g. crossing a Gap). Gravity is world-down but weak, giving floaty hang time.

**Light trail** — Glowing ribbon left behind the Bike: cyan, red where it was laid while Boosting. Purely visual; no collision.

**Checkpoint** — Fixed point on the Track. Leaving the Track (e.g. missing a Gap) respawns the Bike at the last Checkpoint passed, with a time penalty.

**Boost** — Player-triggered burst of speed. The camera's view widens while it lasts.

**Tunnel** — A stretch of Track enclosed by glowing strands and rings arching over it, for a rush of speed. Purely visual; it changes nothing about how the Bike rides.

**Boost pad** — A strip on the Track, marked with chevrons in the Track's secondary colour. Riding onto it gives a speed kick and a Boost that lasts until the strip ends (plus a short tail), without using up the player's own Boost. Each Track sets its strips' lengths to suit its layout.

**Attitude indicator** — HUD gauge showing the Bike's pitch and roll while Airborne.

**Air control** — Player rotation of the Bike while Airborne, to line up a landing.

**Time trial** — The only race mode: one Bike against the clock or a ghost of a previous run. A run is 1, 3 or 5 Laps, picked on Track select; 3 by default.

**Ghost** — A replay of the fastest run on a Track at the current lap count, ridden alongside the current run.

**Pilot** — The headless driver the Track tests ride: full throttle, steering to the centre line, and per Track its own braking, Boost and Air control. Each Track's fastest clean way round is its Pilot Ghost.

**Speed feel** — The rush of speed drawn round the Bike: streaks past the camera while Boosting, a shake on a hard landing or a crash, colours splitting near Boost top speed. Looks only.

**Finish cam** — After the finish line, the camera's slow circle round the stopped Bike behind the Finish screen.

**Trackside scenery** — The neon landmarks round a Track, off the deck: light towers by the Loops and Corkscrews, a beacon tower by each Checkpoint, a holo-board with the Track's name by the start. Looks only.

**Rung ripple** — The rungs lighting up block by block ahead of the Bike in time with the Song, once a bar.

**Ghost line** — Your Ghost's path drawn faintly as dots on the Track ahead of you, showing the line it took. Optional; only for a Ghost of your own.

**Pilot Ghost** — The Ghost while you have no run of your own at a Track and lap count: the Pilot's run, computed when needed, never stored and never in the High scores. Your first finished run replaces it.

**Medal** — Bronze, Silver, Gold or Neon: target times per Track and lap count, set against the Pilot Ghost's run time (Neon beats it). Your Medal is the one your #1 earns.

**Sector** — The stretch of a run between two Checkpoint passes (each Lap's start line counts; the last ends at the finish). A 3-Lap Overdrive run has 12.

**Ideal time** — The best time ever ridden in each Sector at a Track and lap count, added up: what a run of your best Sectors would take.

**Lap** — One full trip round the closed Track, start line to start line.

**Menu** — The screens outside a run: Track select (with the High scores) and Controls. Has its own Song.

**Track select** — Menu screen where the player picks which Track to race and how many Laps, and sees each Track's High scores.

**High scores** — The fastest run times kept per Track and lap count, top five, each with its Lap times, on this device only. The player can reset them. The fastest run is the Ghost.

**Pause** — Stopping a run mid-Lap, with the choice to resume, restart or quit to the Menu.

**Song** — A looping piece of background music. Each Track has its own, and so does the Menu.
